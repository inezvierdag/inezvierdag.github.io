The Grammar of Arrival

Medium: Interactive Installation

Materials: Receipt printer, monitor, projection, local language model, fabric


2026 — Loods6, Amsterdam

The Grammar of Arrival puts a visitor in front of a terminal that can only be answered starting with the word I. Whatever follows is read by a local, offline language model — nothing leaves the room — which scores each word by how surprising it was: a loss value, in nats, measuring the distance between what was written and what the model already expected English to do.

The words the model found most costly are kept; everything else, including the sentence itself, is deleted the moment it is measured. What remains travels onto a curtain of hanging fabric, joining a constellation built by everyone who has written there before — over three days, eighty-six visitors typed 3,653 words into it, and 1,108 of them remained on the wall by closing. A receipt prints the visitor's retained words alongside the model's own continuation: a small, physical account of where a sentence resisted, and where it didn't.

Developed from the artistic research thesis The Loss of a Body: The Measurable Improbability of the Situated Sentence (rMA Art and Performance Studies, University of Amsterdam, 2026), which asks what a language model's loss metric can and cannot register about the body that wrote the sentence it is scoring.

What does a body lose to become legible to a machine?
